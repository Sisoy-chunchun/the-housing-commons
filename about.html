ABOUT THE HOUSING COMMONS.

A shared space for learning, connection, and action around community-owned housing in Nebraska.

About Nebraska Housing Commons

Nebraska Housing Commons is a community-owned and managed digital commons that brings people, knowledge, and resources together to expand community ownership of housing.

The goal is to make participation in shaping the future of housing more accessible. The Commons creates a space where people can share resources, coordinate ideas, solve problems, and work together toward implementation.

Housing problems are complex, but the knowledge and resources needed to address them already exist in our communities. The Commons is an attempt to bring those pieces together and make them easier to find, understand, and use.

Why the Commons Exists

The ethical issue I am focusing on is the commodification of a basic human need. Housing is treated primarily as a private commodity and investment opportunity. The existing housing system in the US creates a conflict between private wealth and the public need for housing stability.

People generally do not have the option to opt out of consuming shelter. This makes housing different from many other products sold in a market. When access to a basic need is also used as a tool for private wealth generation, housing instability becomes more than an individual problem.

My focus shifted away from policy and toward what the community can do to create housing access. Nebraska Housing Commons explores how existing community and public resources could be organized around creating permanent affordable housing and expanding community ownership.

Making Housing Better Through Subtractive Change

The focus of this project is to challenge the existing housing industry by creating community-owned alternatives that prioritize sustainability and the social function of housing.

My approach to making a business better is through subtractive change. I would argue that the existing profit-based model is best improved by challenging its market dominance and contributing to meaningful progress toward developing large-scale alternatives.

This does not mean removing the people and businesses needed to create housing. Housing still requires planning, financing, design, construction, maintenance, management, legal work, accounting, and skilled labor. Those contributions still have value.

The change is in ownership and purpose.

Better means shifting ownership of affordable housing into the hands of communities to create permanently affordable housing systems that prioritize the social function of housing. Instead of allowing the value created through housing to continually leave the community, community ownership creates an opportunity to hold that value and use it for future community benefit.

What Does “Commons” Mean?

The Commons is not just a website. It is shared infrastructure.

I like the idea of creating a space where people can bring existing knowledge and resources together instead of every organization or community having to start from the beginning. There are a lot of organizations working to address affordable housing in creative ways. The Commons would make those efforts easier to see and connect.

The full vision would create a place where people could learn about community ownership and explore projects that already exist. They could find organizations and public resources. They could share research, tools, professional knowledge, land, labor, and ideas.

It would also create space for public discourse and ongoing community involvement. People could propose projects, identify problems encountered during implementation, and work together to find more equitable solutions.

Education would remain a central part of that process. The goal is to create a community-owned resource and shared infrastructure that helps facilitate learning, connections, organization, and the collective building of equitable housing systems.

Who Is the Commons For?

The Commons is for people who want to participate in shaping the future of housing.

That includes people who need housing and people who already work in housing. It includes renters, community members, advocates, nonprofits, public agencies, planners, developers, tradespeople, researchers, students, and community leaders.

It is also for people who have something they are willing to share. That could be professional knowledge, land, labor, research, funding, technology, lived experience, or an idea.

Participation should not require someone to already understand how affordable housing development works. One of the goals of the Commons is to make that knowledge more accessible so more people have an opportunity to participate in the outcome.

About This Prototype

Nebraska Housing Commons is currently a student-developed prototype.

The full vision is much larger than what I can create during one semester. This website is meant to create a structure for the Commons and provide samples of what some of its resources could look like.

The interactive resource map is one example. Educational content and other components of the site are meant to demonstrate how people might learn about existing resources and community-owned housing.

Other ideas presented throughout the site are part of the larger vision. These include a visual network of organizations, shared resources, community project boards, policy resources, collaborative project development, and a community wealth fund.

The prototype is not meant to suggest that all of these systems currently exist. It is meant to make the idea visible.

What Could We Build Together?

My personal experiences with housing instability and homelessness have given me a deep understanding of the systemic consequences of inequality in housing access. They have also shown me that housing and economic security are not intrinsically tied to individual ownership and consumption.

Shared ownership, responsibility, and resources can create stability and support community growth and opportunity.

I believe we all have a social responsibility to each other to drive change, create community ownership, and support social systems committed to addressing housing needs.

Nebraska Housing Commons asks what we could create if we treated housing as something we have a responsibility to build and sustain together.
